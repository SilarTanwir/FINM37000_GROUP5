# PLANNING.md — The Variance Risk Premium in E-mini S&P 500 (ES) Futures Options
---

## 1. Project summary

**Title:** Do options on ES futures cost more than the volatility that follows, and can a delta-hedged option seller collect that premium after costs?

**One-paragraph pitch.** Option prices embed a market-implied forecast of future variance. If investors pay a premium to hold protection against volatility, then on average the variance implied by option prices (the variance swap rate) exceeds the variance that is subsequently realized. We measure this gap for CME E-mini S&P 500 futures options using two complementary methods from the literature, then test whether a seller of delta-hedged options actually keeps the premium once bid/ask spreads, hedging costs and fees are charged.

**Research question.** Does a negative variance risk premium exist in ES futures options, is it consistent across two independent measurement methods, and does it survive realistic trading costs?

**Hypotheses** (hypotheses, not claims from the papers):

| ID | Hypothesis | Test |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Mean realized variance is below the synthetic variance swap rate (VRP < 0) | Carr–Wu measurement, §4.4 |
| H2 | Delta-hedged *long* option gains average below zero; the shortfall is smaller away from the money and larger in high-volatility regimes | Bakshi–Kapadia test, §4.5 |
| H3 | The time series of VRP and the gamma-weighted hedged gains agree in sign and co-move | Cross-check, §4.5 |
| H4 | After costs, a systematic short delta-hedged position still earns a positive average return, with fat left-tail losses | Strategy backtest, §6 stage 7 |

Prior evidence is mixed, so none of these is assumed true. The two source papers study stock index options. Every outcome is reportable: premium exists and survives costs; exists but costs consume it; or the two methods disagree.

## 3. Source papers

### 3.1 Carr & Wu (2009), "Variance Risk Premiums," *Review of Financial Studies* 22(3), 1311–1341
- **What it says:** The risk-neutral expected value of return variance, the variance swap rate, is well approximated by a particular portfolio of options. The variance risk premium is measured as the difference between realized variance and this synthetic swap rate. The paper applies this to five stock indexes and 35 individual stocks (OptionMetrics closing quotes, Jan 1996–Feb 2003). The replication is framed with European options and futures contracts; the jump-induced approximation error is of order $(dF/F)^3$ and is small in their calibrations.
- **What we take:** The model-free option strip for the swap rate; the premium definitions $RV-SW$, $\ln(RV/SW)$ and $RV/SW-1$; synthetic bid/ask/mid swap rates; the 30-day constant-maturity series (linear interpolation in total variance, their Eq. 10, switching to the next two expiries when the nearest is under 8 days); Newey–West inference with 30 lags on the overlapping daily series and 6 lags on non-overlapping monthly series.
- **What we do differently:** ES options on futures; explicit comparison of daily vs 5-minute realized variance (Carr–Wu use daily simple returns, their Eq. 11); the VIX-style discrete strip as the baseline, with Carr–Wu's own implementation (linear interpolation of implied volatility in $\ln(K/F)$ onto a 2,000-point grid spanning ±8 standard deviations, flat extrapolation beyond the quoted strikes, Black–Scholes prices on the grid) kept as a robustness variant; block bootstrap and a cost-aware trading test, which the paper does not do. Note that Carr–Wu already use overlap-robust standard errors, so that is not a point of difference.

### 3.2 Bakshi & Kapadia (2003), "Delta-Hedged Gains and the Negative Market Volatility Risk Premium," *Review of Financial Studies* 16(2), 527–566
- **What it says:** Within a stochastic-volatility framework, the sign and size of the volatility risk premium correspond to the mean delta-hedged portfolio return: the expected gain is driven by the price of volatility risk times option vega, $E[\Pi]=E\int\lambda_u\,\partial C/\partial\sigma_u\,du$ (their Section 1.2, Eqs. 10–12; Proposition 2 gives $E[\Pi]=\lambda\varphi(\tau)S\sigma$ for near-the-money options under Heston-type dynamics), so it is negative only if $\lambda<0$. For S&P 500 index options (1988–1995), the delta-hedged strategy underperforms zero; the underperformance is smaller away from the money and larger when volatility is higher; and it remains after accounting for jump fears.
- **Design details to borrow:** daily observations; quotes taken from the last bid/ask before 3:00 pm CST; moneyness restricted to roughly ±10% (defined as $y=Se^{(r-z)\tau}/K$, which is $F/K$ for futures); maturities between 14 and 60 days, split into 14–30 and 31–60 days; discrete daily rehedging; gains reported in dollars, scaled by the underlying level and scaled by the option price; arbitrage-bound and implied-vol (1%–100%) screens. Their main hedge delta is Black–Scholes evaluated at a GARCH (or rolling sample-standard-deviation) volatility, not the option's own implied volatility. Most of their analysis uses calls; puts are examined separately for out-of-the-money strikes.
- **What we do differently:** the main table in the original reports standard errors as sample standard deviation over the square root of the number of observations, which ignores overlap and same-day correlation. The authors acknowledge this and address it with homogeneous constant-maturity at-the-money series (30, 44, 58 days, non-overlapping) and Bertsimas–Kogan–Lo standardization, and use Newey–West (12 lags) in their regressions. We will report the naive figure and HAC / clustered errors in the full cross-section, and show how much significance changes. We hedge with futures (no financing term on the hedge), use implied-vol deltas as the baseline with the B&K-style realized-vol delta as a robustness variant, and add transaction costs.

### 3.3 Supporting references
- Black (1976), "The pricing of commodity contracts," *Journal of Financial Economics* 3 — for the model for options on futures.
- CBOE VIX methodology white paper — for the discretization and correction term used in practice for the strip.
- Newey & West (1987), *Econometrica* — HAC standard errors.
- Heston & Todorov (2023), "Exploring the Variance Risk Premium Across Assets", *SSRN Electronic Journal*  — closest prior art using futures options
- Databento documentation and its Black-76 Greeks tutorial for CME options.

## 4. Theory

Notation: $F_t$ is the price of the underlying futures contract at $t$; $K$ the strike; $T$ the option expiry; $\tau = (T-t)/365$ (ACT/365, used everywhere for consistency); $r$ the continuously compounded rate; $\sigma$ volatility; $N(\cdot)$ and $\phi(\cdot)$ the standard normal CDF and PDF.

### 4.1 Options on futures: Black-76

For a European option on a futures contract with upfront premium:

$$C = e^{-r\tau}\big[F\,N(d_1) - K\,N(d_2)\big],\qquad P = e^{-r\tau}\big[K\,N(-d_2) - F\,N(-d_1)\big]$$

$$d_1 = \frac{\ln(F/K) + \tfrac12\sigma^2\tau}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau}},\qquad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{\tau}$$

Greeks with respect to $F$:

$$\Delta_C = e^{-r\tau}N(d_1),\quad \Delta_P = \Delta_C - e^{-r\tau},\quad \Gamma = \frac{e^{-r\tau}\phi(d_1)}{F\sigma\sqrt{\tau}},\quad \text{Vega} = F e^{-r\tau}\phi(d_1)\sqrt{\tau}$$

Put–call parity: $C - P = e^{-r\tau}(F-K)$.

**Implied volatility** is the $\sigma$ that makes the model price equal the market price. It is solved numerically (bracketed root-finding; Brent) and must reject prices below intrinsic value or above the no-arbitrage upper bound.

**Exercise style.** Black-76 is exact only for European exercise. Since we will use a monthly_eom series, we restrict our analysis to European options. 

**Forward price.** Instead of trusting a single futures print, estimate $F$ from option quotes via parity at the strike where $|C-P|$ is smallest (the VIX approach), and compare it with the futures mid at the same timestamp. Non-synchronous option and futures prices are a major source of spurious implied-volatility noise.

### 4.2 Realized variance

For log returns $r_i = \ln(F_{t_i}/F_{t_{i-1}})$ of the **specific futures contract that underlies the option** over $[t,T]$:

$$RV_{t,T} = \frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{n} r_i^2$$

Dividing by calendar-year $\tau$ keeps $RV$ on the same annualization basis as implied variance. Variants:
- **Daily:** settlement-to-settlement returns. (Carr–Wu's Eq. 11 uses simple percentage returns and an annualization of 365/30 over the 30-day window; they report that log vs simple returns, demeaning, and ACT/365 vs 252 day count do not change conclusions. We use log returns in the baseline and treat simple returns as a robustness variant.)
- **Intraday:** 5-minute returns. Higher frequency captures more information but is more exposed to microstructure noise; 5 minutes is the conventional compromise.
- ES trades nearly around the clock. The session definition (full Globex vs regular hours) and the treatment of the daily maintenance gap must be explicit in the config **[DECISION]**.

### 4.3 Variance swaps and the model-free strip

A variance swap pays $RV_{t,T} - K_{var}$ at expiry and costs nothing to enter, so $K_{var}$, the **swap rate** $SW_{t,T}$, is the risk-neutral expectation $\mathbb{E}^Q[RV_{t,T}]$.

**Derivation sketch (continuous paths).** By Itô's lemma, $d\ln F = dF/F - \tfrac12 d\langle F\rangle/F^2$, so

$$\int_t^T d\langle \ln F\rangle = 2\!\int_t^T \frac{dF}{F} - 2\ln\frac{F_T}{F_t}.$$

Futures prices are $Q$-martingales, so the first term has zero expectation and $\mathbb{E}^Q[\int d\langle\ln F\rangle] = -2\,\mathbb{E}^Q[\ln(F_T/F_t)]$. The log contract is spanned by options:

$$\ln\frac{F_T}{F_0} = \frac{F_T-F_0}{F_0} - \int_0^{F_0}\frac{(K-F_T)^+}{K^2}dK - \int_{F_0}^{\infty}\frac{(F_T-K)^+}{K^2}dK$$

Taking $Q$-expectations and using $\mathbb{E}^Q[(\cdot)^+] = e^{r\tau}\times$ price gives the swap rate as a strip of out-of-the-money options weighted by $1/K^2$.

**Discrete implementation (VIX-style):**

$$SW_{t,T} = \frac{2}{\tau}e^{r\tau}\sum_i \frac{\Delta K_i}{K_i^2}\,Q(K_i) \;-\; \frac{1}{\tau}\Big(\frac{F}{K_0}-1\Big)^2$$

- $K_0$: highest strike at or below $F$.
- $Q(K_i)$: OTM put for $K_i<K_0$, OTM call for $K_i>K_0$, average of put and call at $K_0$.
- $\Delta K_i = (K_{i+1}-K_{i-1})/2$ (one-sided at the edges).
- Use bid/ask **mid** for the baseline; compute bid-based and ask-based strips as bounds (Carr–Wu report variants from bid and ask prices).

**Known approximation errors (must be diagnosed, not ignored):**
1. *Truncation:* finite strike range omits tail contributions (biases $SW$ downward).
2. *Discretization:* coarse strikes (error shrinks as the grid refines).
3. *Jumps:* exact replication holds for continuous paths; with jumps the strip is an approximation (the source paper calls it "well approximated").
4. *Stale or crossed quotes* far OTM.
5. *Maturity mismatch:* the strip prices variance to a specific expiry.

**Maturity handling.** Baseline: match the realized-variance horizon to each expiry's actual $\tau$ (no interpolation). Also report, as the series directly comparable to Carr–Wu, a constant 30-day maturity by linear interpolation in total variance between the two expiries bracketing 30 days ($SW_{t,T}=\frac{1}{T-t}\big[SW_{T_1}(T_1-t)\tfrac{T_2-T}{T_2-T_1}+SW_{T_2}(T_2-t)\tfrac{T-T_1}{T_2-T_1}\big]$), using the next two expiries when the nearest is within 8 days (Carr–Wu's rule). The realized-variance leg for that series is the 30-day window.

**Alternative implementation (robustness).** Carr–Wu do not use the discrete VIX sum. They interpolate implied volatility linearly in $k=\ln(K/F)$, extend it flat beyond the quoted strikes, build a 2,000-point strike grid spanning ±8 standard deviations (standard deviation approximated by the average implied vol), price OTM options with Black–Scholes on that grid and integrate Eq. (5). This avoids the truncation bias of the discrete sum but replaces it with an extrapolation assumption. Implement as `strip.method: iv_grid` next to the baseline `vix_discrete`.

### 4.4 The variance risk premium

$$VRP_{t,T} = RV_{t,T} - SW_{t,T}\qquad(\text{variance units; Carr–Wu})$$

Also reported: the log variance risk premium $LRP=\ln(RV/SW)$ and the variance-swap excess return $RV/SW - 1$ for a long-variance position (Carr–Wu use the log form to make the distribution closer to normal; their Sharpe ratio for shorting variance is the mean of $-\ln(RV/SW)$ over its Newey–West standard deviation, annualized by $\sqrt{365/30}$). A negative mean means variance buyers pay a premium and variance sellers earn it. $SW_{t,T}$ is measured at $t$; $RV_{t,T}$ is realized afterwards, so the pair is only observable at $T$ (careful with look-ahead in any regime variable).

### 4.5 Delta-hedged gains and the link to the VRP

For an option bought at $t_0$ for $O_0$ and hedged by shorting $\Delta_n$ futures at discrete times $t_0<\dots<t_{N-1}$, with $t_N=T$:

$$\Pi_{t_0,T} = O_T - e^{r\tau}O_0 - \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\Delta_n\,(F_{t_{n+1}}-F_{t_n})$$

- $O_T$ is intrinsic value at expiry; the premium is financed at $r$; futures hold no financing term because they are marked to market.
- A **seller** earns $-\Pi$.
- Report $\Pi$ in dollars, $\Pi/F_{t_0}$, and $\Pi/O_0$, as in Bakshi–Kapadia.

**Why it measures the premium.** If hedging uses the same implied volatility $\sigma_i$ used to price the option, a standard Black–Scholes hedging result gives, up to financing terms,

$$\Pi \approx \tfrac12\sum_n \Gamma_n F_n^2\big(r_{n+1}^2 - \sigma_i^2\,\Delta t_n\big)$$

i.e. a gamma-weighted version of "realized minus implied variance". **This gamma-weighted identity is the standard Black–Scholes hedging result, not a result of either source paper.** Bakshi–Kapadia's own link is different: under stochastic volatility the mean hedged gain equals the (vega-weighted) price of volatility risk, $E[\Pi]=E\int\lambda_u\,\partial C/\partial\sigma_u\,du$, so the sign of the mean gain reveals the sign of the premium, the effect is largest at the money (where vega is largest), and, for near-the-money options, scales with the level of volatility. Carr–Wu measure the premium at the variance level via the swap strip. H3 therefore tests whether two related but differently weighted objects (gamma-weighted hedged gains and $RV-SW$) agree in sign and co-move; it is not a restatement of a proven equality. 

**Hedge delta choices (baseline vs robustness):** (a) delta from the option's own implied vol, recomputed daily from that day's quotes; (b) delta from the entry implied vol held fixed; (c) delta from a trailing realized-vol estimate (closest to Bakshi–Kapadia, who use Black–Scholes delta at a GARCH volatility, with a rolling sample-standard-deviation alternative).

**Moneyness and regimes.**
- Moneyness $m = \ln(K/F_t)$; baseline sample $|m|\le 10\%$; report buckets (e.g. OTM puts, near-ATM, OTM calls).
- Regime variables must use information available at $t$ only: the swap-rate level $SW_t$ and/or trailing 21-day realized volatility, classified by **expanding-window** percentiles (never full-sample percentiles).

### 4.6 Inference with overlapping windows

Daily-start, ~30-day-horizon observations overlap, so errors are serially correlated; options on the same day share the same shock, so they are cross-sectionally correlated.

- **Primary:** Newey–West HAC standard errors with lag at least the horizon in trading days minus one. For comparability with the sources, also report 30 lags on the overlapping daily series (Carr–Wu) and a short lag on non-overlapping monthly series (Carr–Wu use 6; Bakshi–Kapadia use 12 in their time-series regressions).
- **Cross-section:** cluster by entry date.
- **Alternative:** non-overlapping monthly observations (fewer, cleaner; the Bakshi–Kapadia constant-maturity design), and a block bootstrap.
- Always report the naive standard error alongside, to quantify the overstatement.

### 4.7 Costs and risk metrics

- **Option costs:** buy at the ask, sell at the bid (half-spread each way versus mid). Parameterize by moneyness bucket from a quote-based calibration sample.
- **Hedge costs:** ES futures slippage per rebalance, plus exchange, clearing and broker fees per contract (config parameters).
- **Capital:** short options require margin; report returns on a stated notional and, as a secondary view, on an assumed margin fraction **[DECISION]**.
- **Risk metrics:** annualized Sharpe, skewness, worst day/month, max drawdown, and conditional value at risk. Short volatility has negative skew; the average gain can hide rare large losses, so the tails must be displayed, not averaged away.

### 4.8 Limitations to state up front
- Short samples (2–3 years of tick-quality data) can be dominated by the presence or absence of a single volatility spike. Use longer daily settlement data for the premium measurement if the license allows.
- The model assumes continuous paths and constant-parameter deltas; jumps and stochastic volatility mean hedges are imperfect by construction.
- Quotes far from the money are stale and wide; mid-based strips are sensitive to them.
- Exercise-style mismatch (§4.1).

## 9. Testing strategy (all tests run offline)

| Test | What it proves |
|---|---|
| `test_black76` | Put–call parity; price→IV round-trip; delta/gamma match finite differences; rejects arbitrage-violating prices |
| `test_strip_flat_vol` | On a dense strike grid priced at constant $\sigma$, $SW\approx\sigma^2$; error shrinks with finer grid; narrow strike range shows downward truncation bias |
| `test_realized_variance` | On simulated GBM, daily and 5-minute RV both converge to $\sigma^2$; gap handling is correct |
| `test_hedge_zero_premium` | If simulated realized vol equals the pricing vol, mean delta-hedged gain ≈ 0 within Monte Carlo error; if realized > implied, a long hedged option gains on average |
| `test_hedge_identity` (optional) | Hedged gain ≈ gamma-weighted $(RV-\sigma_i^2)$ on simulated paths |
| `test_inference` | On a simulated overlapping AR series, HAC SE exceeds naive SE |
| `test_regimes_no_lookahead` | Labels at time $t$ are unchanged when later data is appended |
| `test_chain_builder` | Fixture definitions parse into the right strike/expiry/type/underlying; cleaning rules behave |

## 13. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| License or budget does not cover enough option history | Medium | Check first (#3); shorten sample; use settlements/daily for measurement and snapshots only for hedging |
| Wrong or noisy strikes/quotes | Medium | Strike sanity check; cleaning log; synthetic tests |
| Exercise-style mismatch | Medium | Choose a European series or document error |
| Sample dominated by one volatility episode | High | Report sub-samples; state limitation; use a longer daily sample if possible |
| Scope creep (more products, intraday hedging) | High | Out-of-scope list below; stretch issue only after #19 |
| Team member bottleneck on tricky modules (#9, #12) | Medium | Pair on L-size issues; write tests first |
| Result is null or contradicts the papers | Medium | Acceptable and reportable; no tuning until a result appears |
